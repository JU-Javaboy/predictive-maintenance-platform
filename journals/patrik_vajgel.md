My task for sprint review 1 was to focus on sensor drift. I was not familiar with this concept, so I took some time to research it, and then analyze the data. As far as I am aware, sensor drift is the concept of a change in calibration for a sensor, which leads to different readings over time. The dataset consists of 3 different numeric measurements: temperature, vibration, and rpm. I started by investigating changes in temperature and vibration over time. I noticed that they steadily increase over time in a cycle that lasts around a month, then reset to some baseline. I suspected that this was not a case of sensor drift, but a change in data over time. I then noticed that this was also the case with pressure and power. Then I realized that this was simulating a scheduled maintenance, when remaining useful hours dropped below some value. I have therefore decided to leave it be for now, and gain insight from the sprint review.

RPM was a bit more tricky. When graphing it, I immediately noticed 2 sudden jumps in distribution. This is where the bulk of my time went: how do I detect this jump? I looked into the KS test, a way to see if 2 different distributions align, I researched the EMWA, a way to calculate averages with the most recent measurement having a higher priority. However, I realised I might have overcomplicating it a bit. I realized that normalization might be the answer: assuming that the first period (the one before the first jump) is the baseline, then we can simply calculate the std, and mean from this. Then we can split the dataset into the 2 jumps, calculate their std and mean, normalize them, but then unnormalize it using the baseline's std and mean. This basically changes the distribution of the measurements back to the baseline. Assuming this is the correct approach, a simple procedure will be introduced.
